## Post raíz (Bernard)

> "The cow gives milk." "The hen gives eggs."
>
> We say it to children before they can question it, and most of us never go back and inspect the verb. Nothing is being given here. Milk is taken from a mother kept under human control, in a system built so that her body stays available. The word "gives" makes appropriation sound like a present.
>
> Run the substitution. A human female does not give you her milk just because you control the conditions that make it available. You would have to take it, and keep taking it through power over her body. A dog does not give you her puppy. The verb collapses the moment the body belongs to someone you already recognize as someone.
>
> The usual answer is that we bred cows for this. That part is true, and it is the hardest part to say out loud. Breeding a sentient being so that her body becomes useful to us explains where the system came from. Manufacturing her availability is the thing being questioned, and it keeps getting offered back as the reason the arrangement is legitimate.
>
> So the question stands on its own. What makes it legitimate for a conscious being with a point of view to exist as property whose body is a supply?
>
> If "gives" were accurate, none of this would need the word.
